Three case studies

Each one says what the problem was, what I did, what changed, and where the evidence is. Figures are as stated on my CV and the linked dashboards.

Quality: line first pass yield from an average of 58% to 92%Caravan production line

The problem

Line first pass yield averaged 58% from September 2024 to March 2025, and fell to 43% in September 2024.

What I did

  • Gave shop-floor teams direct ownership of quality.
  • Ran daily reporting and root-cause investigation on production scrap, and turned the findings into corrective actions.
  • Audited SOPs and QVAs to 97.7% critical process coverage.

What changed

  • Line first pass yield reached 92% in July 2025 (to the 15th), with individual weeks at 100%. The tracker ends on 15 July 2025, so it does not show whether this held.
  • Vehicle defects down 40%.
  • Damaged-in-Production scrap costs 17.7% under the annual budget.

The evidence

  • DMAIC and first pass yield board
  • 5 Whys root cause tool
  • SPC control chart tool
People: skills cover from 36% to 86.6%Team of 29, 260 processes

The problem

Skills cover was 36%, with single points of failure across the 260 processes the team ran.

What I did

  • Built and kept a bay-level competency matrix covering all 260 processes.
  • Designed and led a cross-functional 3x3 training programme.

What changed

  • Team skills cover from 36% to 86.6% in two years, with 44.6% cross-coverage (116 of all 260 processes with three or more trained operators) and single points of failure removed.
  • 11 of the 29 team members fully skilled on the matrix.
  • 3x3 cross-training: 85 of 86 critical-to-quality SOPs have three or more trained operators (98.8%), up from 70% in September 2025. This includes all 36 in Bays 5 to 8.

The evidence

  • Skills matrix and cross-training dashboard
  • Skills matrix builder, with sign-offs that expire
  • Company skills dashboard
Safety: from compliance to ownershipWith the Head of Health and Safety

The problem

The aim was to move the team from compliance to genuine safety ownership.

What I did

  • Worked with the Head of Health and Safety, and co-implemented SafetyCube, an EHS management system.
  • Raised 108 of the 324 site reports logged from April 2025 to March 2026 myself (102 near misses and hazards, 6 accident reports), 33% of the total.
  • Carried out ergonomic (QEC) risk assessments and daily PUWER machine checks.

What changed

  • 324 reports logged in the first year, from April 2025 to March 2026, raised by 22 people.
  • Top reporter on site: 108 reports, against 52 for the next highest.
  • My own reports rose from 19 in April to June 2025 to 45 in October to December 2025.

The evidence

  • EHS readiness dashboard
  • Incident and near-miss log
  • Risk assessment dashboard

How I build these

  • Gaps are shown, not hidden. A tool says "Gaps found. Do not rely on this" when its checks fail, and "No gaps found by these checks" only when they pass. It never says something is safe or compliant.
  • Example data is labelled. Anything that is not a real figure says so on the page.
  • Figures say where they come from. Each result names its source, such as my tracker, my CV or my scrap document, and notes what has not been checked against company records.
  • Your data stays with you. Every tool runs in your browser with no sign-in. Nothing is sent to me.
