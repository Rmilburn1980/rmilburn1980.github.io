Line Balancing

Enter the timed work elements for a line, put each one at a station, and see every station's load against takt on a yamazumi chart. It shows balance efficiency, the slowest station, and what is missing from the record. It does not check the order the work has to be done in.

What the checks found

The checks look for gaps in the record: elements with no time, no class or no station, too few timings, stations with nothing in them, and no independent check. They cannot tell whether the times are true, whether the method is the best one, or whether moving an element is possible. The tool does not check the order the work must be done in.

What it measures

  • Takt is available time divided by demand, in seconds. It is how often a unit must be finished to meet demand.
  • Station time is the total of the element times at that station. A station over takt cannot keep up on its own.
  • Balance efficiency is total element time divided by the number of stations times the slowest station's time. 100% would mean every station takes the same time. It says nothing about whether the line meets takt.
  • Balance delay is 100% minus balance efficiency: the share of station time spent waiting on the slowest station.
  • Fewest stations at this takt is total element time divided by takt, rounded up. It is a floor from the arithmetic, not a design. The real answer is often higher because of the order of the work.
  • Output at the slowest station is available time divided by the slowest station's time, rounded down. Compare it with demand.

The rules the tool applies

  • Time what the work actually takes, several times, and record how many timings you took. Fewer than three is noted. Use the time you observed, not a standard allowance.
  • Every element needs a class: value-adding, necessary but not value-adding, or waste. An element with no class is shown in grey and flagged.
  • An element with no station is left out of the station times and is counted as unassigned. An element with no usable time is left out entirely. Both are flagged, never counted as zero.
  • A station with no elements is flagged and still counts as a station, so it lowers balance efficiency.
  • A station under 70% of takt is noted. It may have room for more work.
  • The times should be timed by one person and checked by another. If they are the same, or blank, it is flagged.

Moving work between stations

Change the station of an element from the table and the chart updates. The tool does not know the order work must be done in, what equipment each station has, or who is trained, so a move that looks good on the chart may not be possible. Check it against the method, the safety of the new task and the people who will do it before you change anything on the line.
