What the checks found

The checks look for gaps in the record. They cannot tell whether a sign-off was a fair judgement of the person's skill.

Levels

  • 0 Not trained. Has not been taught the task.
  • 1 In training. Being taught. Always with a trainer.
  • 2 Needs supervision. Can do parts, someone must be nearby.
  • 3 Competent alone. Does the task to standard without help. Needs a sign-off.
  • 4 Can train others. Competent, and has shown they can teach it. Needs a sign-off.

The rules the tool applies

  • A level 3 or 4 counts as covered only when it has an assessor, a date, the assessor is not the operator, and the date is within the valid period.
  • Anything else at level 3 or 4 is shown with a dashed outline and left out of the coverage count.
  • A critical skill with fewer signed-off people than you need is a gap. With none, it says so plainly.
  • A non-critical skill short of its target is a note.
  • A critical skill with no signed-off trainer at level 4 is a note.
  • A training date that has passed for someone still below level 3 is a gap.
  • A critical skill with no SOP number recorded is a note. Sign off against the written standard, and put its number in the matrix.
  • A person who is the only signed-off cover for three or more skills is listed as a key person risk.

Good practice

  • Sign off by watching the person do the task, not by asking them. Write down what you saw, and the date.
  • Review sign-offs when the process, the equipment or the standard changes, as well as on the date.
  • Train for critical skills first, then spread cover so that a holiday or a sickness does not stop the line.
  • Use the matrix to plan training. Do not use it on its own to judge a person.

Limits

No gaps found does not mean the team is competent. The tool only checks the record. A competent person has to judge whether the sign-offs are fair and whether the skills listed are the ones the job needs. Check your own legal duties on training and competence.
